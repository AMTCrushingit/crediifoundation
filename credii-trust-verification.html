Trust & Verification Policy

Effective Date: October 20, 2026 · Last Updated: October 20, 2026

Regulatory and Privacy Alignment. Credii Foundation is established in Trinidad & Tobago. This Policy has been developed with regard to the privacy and information-protection principles reflected in Trinidad & Tobago’s Data Protection Act, 2011, together with Credii’s commitment to responsible data stewardship, transparency, security and individual agency. As Credii expands its services across the Caribbean, its trust and verification practices will continue to be reviewed against applicable legal and regulatory requirements in the jurisdictions in which it operates.

1. Purpose

Credii Foundation (“Credii”) is building trust infrastructure designed to help people make their achievements, skills, experiences, participation and other evidence of capability easier to verify, carry forward and use when pursuing opportunities.

Trust is central to Credii's mission.

This Trust & Verification Policy explains:

  • what Credii means by “verified”;
  • how information may be verified;
  • who may provide verification;
  • how verified information may appear within Credii;
  • how corrections and disputes are handled;
  • the responsibilities of members and participating organizations; and
  • the limits of Credii's verification and trust services.

Verification does not mean that Credii guarantees an individual, organization, outcome or opportunity. It means that specific information has been checked against an identified source or evidence using Credii's applicable verification process.

2. Our Trust Principles

Credii's approach to trust is guided by five principles.

  • Evidence before assumption. Trust signals should be supported by identifiable evidence, records or authorized sources wherever verification is claimed.
  • Transparency. Users should be able to understand what has been verified, who or what provided the verification, and the status of the information.
  • Proportionality. The level of verification should be appropriate to the type of information being verified.
  • Individual agency. People should have meaningful visibility into the information associated with their Credii profile and processes for challenging inaccurate information.
  • Trust is contextual. No single credential, achievement, endorsement or score defines a person's capability or trustworthiness.

Credii is designed to bring multiple pieces of verified evidence together, not to reduce an individual to a single data point.

3. What Credii May Verify

Depending on the services and verification methods available, Credii may support verification of information such as:

  • education and training;
  • credentials and certificates;
  • program completion;
  • skills development activities;
  • employment or professional experience;
  • volunteer and community participation;
  • entrepreneurship and business-development activities;
  • professional memberships;
  • awards and achievements;
  • references or endorsements; and
  • other forms of participation, experience or accomplishment supported by appropriate evidence.

The availability and method of verification may differ depending on the type of information, issuing organization and available evidence.

4. Sources of Verification

Information may be verified through one or more approved sources. These may include:

  • Issuing Organizations. Schools, training providers, nonprofits, professional bodies, employers, program operators or other organizations may confirm records they issued or maintain.
  • Authorized Representatives. An authorized representative of an organization may confirm a participant's achievement, participation or other relevant record.
  • Documentary Evidence. Credii may use appropriate supporting documentation as part of a verification process.
  • Independent Sources. Where appropriate and available, information may be checked against an authoritative or independent source.

Credii may establish additional verification methods as the platform evolves.

5. Verification Status

Credii should clearly distinguish between information that has been verified and information that has not. Depending on the platform functionality available, records may carry statuses such as:

  • Verified. The information has completed the applicable Credii verification process.
  • Pending Verification. Verification has been requested or initiated but has not been completed.
  • Unverified. The information has not been independently verified through the applicable Credii process.
  • Unable to Verify. Credii or an authorized verifier could not obtain sufficient evidence to complete verification.
  • Disputed. The accuracy, ownership or validity of the information has been challenged and may require further review.
  • Revoked or Withdrawn. A previously verified record has subsequently been withdrawn, revoked or determined no longer to meet the applicable verification requirements.

Credii may refine these categories as the Trust Framework and platform mature.

6. What “Verified” Means

A Credii verification applies to the specific record or information evaluated. For example, verification of completion of a training program means that the applicable verification process confirmed evidence of that program completion.

It does not automatically mean that Credii:

  • endorses the participant;
  • guarantees the participant's competence;
  • guarantees future performance;
  • guarantees the accuracy of unrelated information;
  • recommends the individual for employment, financing or another opportunity; or
  • guarantees any decision made by a third party.

Verification is one trust signal among potentially many.

7. Trust Profiles

Credii may bring verified and other permitted information together within a Trust Profile. A Trust Profile may include different categories of evidence and trust signals. The purpose is to make credible evidence of a person's achievements, capabilities and contributions easier to understand and carry across opportunities.

Where information has not been verified, Credii should not present that information in a way that could reasonably be mistaken for verified information.

8. Trust Indicators and Future Trust Scoring

Credii may develop indicators, frameworks or methodologies that help users and organizations understand the strength, source or status of available trust evidence.

The existence of a Trust Profile does not mean that Credii has assigned an overall judgment regarding an individual's character, financial reliability, employability or general trustworthiness.

If Credii introduces a formal Trust Score or similar assessment in the future, Credii intends to establish and publish separate methodology and governance requirements before such scoring is used for decision-making.

This distinction is important: Credii verifies evidence. Credii does not currently declare whether a person is inherently “trustworthy.”

9. Responsibilities of Members

Members are responsible for:

  • providing truthful and accurate information;
  • submitting authentic documents and evidence;
  • obtaining any required permission before submitting information relating to another person;
  • keeping account information secure;
  • notifying Credii of known inaccuracies; and
  • not misrepresenting a verification status.

Submitting falsified, manipulated or misleading information may result in:

  • rejection of a verification request;
  • removal of the affected record;
  • suspension of verification status; or
  • suspension or termination of platform access.

10. Responsibilities of Participating Organizations

Organizations participating in Credii verification processes are expected to:

  • provide accurate information;
  • designate appropriately authorized representatives;
  • verify only information the organization is reasonably able to confirm;
  • protect participant information;
  • promptly notify Credii of discovered errors;
  • cooperate with appropriate dispute or correction processes; and
  • avoid issuing or confirming misleading records.

Participation in Credii does not authorize an organization to verify information outside the organization's appropriate authority or knowledge.

11. Corrections and Disputes

Credii recognizes that records can contain mistakes and that verified information can later be challenged. Members and participating organizations may request review of information they believe is incorrect. Credii may, depending on the circumstances:

  1. review the request;
  2. request supporting information;
  3. contact the original verifier or issuing organization;
  4. temporarily mark the record as disputed;
  5. correct inaccurate information;
  6. restore verification where evidence supports it; or
  7. withdraw verification where sufficient evidence cannot be established.

Credii should maintain an appropriate record of material verification status changes.

12. Fraud and Misrepresentation

Credii may investigate suspected misuse of its verification services. Verification may be refused, suspended or withdrawn where Credii identifies evidence suggesting that information, documentation or representations are false, materially misleading or improperly submitted. Where necessary, access to Credii services may also be restricted.

13. Privacy and Data Protection

Verification can involve personal information. Credii will seek to collect and use personal information only where reasonably necessary for legitimate platform, verification, program, research or administrative purposes and will apply appropriate safeguards to information under its control.

Trinidad & Tobago's Data Protection Act, 2011 establishes a statutory framework for protecting personal privacy and information. The Act includes provisions addressing matters such as personal information, accuracy and protection of information.

Credii's handling of personal information is further addressed in the Credii Foundation Privacy Policy.

14. Data Integrity and Security

Credii will seek to maintain reasonable technical, administrative and organizational safeguards appropriate to the nature of information processed through the platform. Verification records should be protected against unauthorized access, alteration, disclosure or loss. Access to verification capabilities may be restricted according to user role and authorization.

15. Technology and Verification Integrity

Credii may use technology to assist with verification, record management, fraud detection, evidence review or other trust-related processes. Technology may support verification, but automated processes should not automatically be treated as conclusive evidence of truth.

Credii may introduce additional verification technologies, standards or methods as the platform develops. Any such technology should support the integrity of the verification process rather than replace appropriate oversight.

16. Artificial Intelligence

Credii may use artificial intelligence to support platform operations, analysis or administrative processes. Where AI is introduced into trust-related processes, Credii's approach will be guided by transparency, human oversight, data protection and the principle that consequential determinations about individuals should not be presented as objective facts merely because they were generated by an automated system. Additional AI governance standards may be adopted as Credii's technology evolves.

17. No Guarantee of Opportunity

A verified record or Credii Trust Profile does not guarantee:

  • employment;
  • admission;
  • financing;
  • investment;
  • grants;
  • contracts;
  • program acceptance;
  • professional licensing; or
  • any other opportunity.

Opportunity providers remain responsible for their own eligibility criteria, assessments and decisions. Credii provides trust infrastructure. It does not make decisions on behalf of employers, financial institutions, funders or other opportunity providers unless explicitly stated for a specific Credii-operated program.

18. Continuous Improvement

Credii's Trust & Verification Framework is expected to evolve as the platform is tested with participants and institutions across the Caribbean. Credii may update verification methods, evidence requirements, terminology, dispute procedures, trust indicators, technology, security controls and governance standards based on pilot findings, research, regulatory requirements and emerging good practices. Material changes to this Policy will be reflected through updated publication dates.
